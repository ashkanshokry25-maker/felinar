Open source, MIT license. Version 0.3.0, pre-release.

Websites loaded by magnet link, verified in your browser, from a plain static host. No backend. The light web.

No backend means there's no application server or database for you to run. You still need an HTTPS static host, which serves the loader on every visit and keeps the site loading when nobody else is online. Visitors find each other through WebSocket trackers and STUN servers, public ones or your own.

Shared data lives in the site ledger: a signed, hash-linked log that every visitor copies and verifies. No mining, no consensus, no finality.

Why felinar

Every benefit below comes with the condition it depends on. The long version, with the numbers and their sources, is in WHY.md.

  • Hosting that costs next to nothing

    Your site stays a folder of files on a plain static host: GitHub Pages, Cloudflare Pages or a bucket behind a CDN. Visitors who choose to share pass the files on to each other.

    Condition: peers carry a real share of the bytes only for large sites with many visitors online at once. A site under about 16 MiB gets about 0% of its first load from peers (a model, not a measurement). Choose it for resilience, not to cut a small bill.

  • Shared data without a backend

    Guestbooks, comments and notices live in the site ledger, moderated with your keys. Your pages use it through mw.db: create, read, update and delete, with no database to run.

    Condition: everything written is public and there's no finality. New entries spread only while someone with a copy is online, so keep a snapshot on your host or a pinned node.

  • Your domain and your key stay in charge

    The site is addressed by its content, and a pointer signed with your owner key names the current version. Browsers check every piece and remember your key from the first visit.

    Condition: your HTTPS host and your DNS are still the trust root. Whoever controls them controls what visitors run.

  • One script tag for an existing site

    Drop-in mode leaves your HTML on your host, so URLs, redirects, 404 pages and search results stay as they are. If the runtime fails, pages load from the host as before.

    Condition: new visitors still need your host, and filling each visitor's copy can multiply your host's traffic. The publish tool prints the figure for your site.

  • Returning visitors ride out an outage

    In shell mode the browser keeps a verified copy and serves it at your site's own URLs, online or offline.

    Condition: only for visitors who loaded the site before and still hold the copy; Safari may clear it after 7 days without a visit. Crawlers see the loader, not your pages.

  • No tracking added by the loader

    No analytics, no telemetry and no third-party scripts. Nothing connects to a peer, tracker, STUN server or DNS-over-HTTPS service until the visitor chooses, and "Load from host only" sits right beside "Share and load".

    Condition: your host still sees each visitor's IP address, as any web host does. Once a visitor shares, other visitors and the connection servers can see their IP address and that they're viewing your site.

How it works

  1. Loader

    Every visit downloads a small loader from your HTTPS host.

  2. Pointer

    The loader checks the signature on the site's pointer, a tag in the page or a DNS record read over DNS-over-HTTPS, and on later visits that it carries the key remembered on the first.

  3. Pieces

    Files come from your host's web seed and, if the visitor agrees, from other visitors over WebRTC. Trackers and STUN servers only introduce browsers. Every piece is checked against the infohash.

  4. Copy

    The verified files and the site ledger are kept in the browser's storage.

  5. Serve

    A Service Worker answers your site's own URLs from that copy, so links, fetch('/data.json') and client-side routers work unchanged. In drop-in mode your host still serves the HTML.

  6. Ledger

    Your pages read and write shared data with mw.db. Entries are signed, carry a small spam fee and travel to other visitors over the same connections.

Shell mode

For new or peer-first sites. Your site runs in a same-URL frame, from its torrent. Returning visitors keep working when the host is down.

Search engines and link previews see the loader, not your content.

Drop-in mode

For existing sites. One script tag; your HTML stays on your host. After the first page view, the other files come from the visitor's verified copy.

The host stays in the loop for every new visitor.

Shared data in a few lines

Publish with an open writing policy, then any page of the site can write to its ledger and react to every visitor's entries. Each write is signed with the visitor's own key and pays a small proof-of-work fee against spam.

Everything written with mw.db is public and copied to every visitor. Deleting an entry hides it for visitors who receive the deletion.

The data guide and the SDK reference cover the rest.

import mw from '/_mw/sdk.js';
await mw.ready();

// Read: now and after every change
mw.db.subscribe(({ lists }) => {
  render(lists.guestbook || []);
});

// Create: signed, with a spam fee
await mw.db.append('guestbook', {
  msg: 'Hello!',
});

// Delete one of your own entries
await mw.db.tombstone(entry.h);

What it is not

  • Not free of servers. Every visit downloads the loader from your HTTPS host, and trackers and STUN servers introduce visitors to each other.
  • Not a cryptocurrency chain. No mining, no consensus, no finality. The proof-of-work on each post is a spam fee, not security.
  • Not anonymous. Peers, trackers and STUN servers see each visitor's IP address. There's no private mode.
  • Not a backup. Browsers can clear their copy. Your host's files, a ledger snapshot and a pinned node are the floor.
  • Not for private data. Accounts, payments, email and secrets stay on a server.

Get started

You need Node 22 or newer and git. The demo builds the loader and the identity page, publishes an example guestbook, starts a local tracker, a dev server and the identity page, and prints the address to open. Open it in two different browsers, choose "Share and load" in both, and sign the guestbook in one: the post signs you in at the identity page first, and the entry appears in the other.

Status: version 0.3.0 passes its unit and end-to-end suites on one development machine, over loopback only. The release gate on real phones and browsers hasn't run yet, so nothing here is called production-ready.
