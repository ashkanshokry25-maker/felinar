### F1. The live public set on 2026-10-17 is the same four trackers, and none failed on its own account in a 2-hour soak `[DESIGN-CRITICAL]`

**Claim.** `wss://tracker.webtorrent.dev`, `wss://tracker.openwebtorrent.com`, `wss://open.ftorrent.com` and `wss://tracker.novage.com.ua` are the only general-purpose public WebSocket trackers that pass a full signalling test; three days after the first wave nothing changed, and across the soak window below no sample of any of them failed except one (21:30:06Z) where three of them timed out together during my own local load test - excluded below with the reason, raw data untouched. The dead and restricted entries of the first wave are unchanged (`tracker.btorrent.xyz` still refuses connections; `tracker.files.fm:7073` still answers 403 to foreign origins).

**Method (re-runnable).** `probes/soak.py` (stdlib Python) every 5 minutes: DNS, TCP, TLS, WebSocket upgrade with `Origin: https://example.org`, then one `announce` with `numwant: 0` and no offers on a random never-published info hash (a health check must not use `scrape`: openwebtorrent closes the socket on it); every 15 minutes additionally a 2-peer offer->answer relay in a private random swarm; one GET of each operator's public stats endpoint. Load on third parties: at most 3 short connections per tracker per 5 minutes. Local observer: one residential/corporate IPv4 address, one machine; no browser, no mobile network. This is liveness and protocol correctness, **not** a measure of user-perceived reliability from many networks.
