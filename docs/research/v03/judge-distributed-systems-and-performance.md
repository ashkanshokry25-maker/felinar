# Judge: distributed systems and performance lens (v0.3 identity, chat and files)

- **Date:** 2026-10-20. **Judged:** [IDENTITY-AUTH-DESIGN.md](IDENTITY-AUTH-DESIGN.md) (cited "ID") and [REALTIME-AND-FILES-DESIGN.md](REALTIME-AND-FILES-DESIGN.md) (cited "RT"), against the goals in [DIFFERENTIATORS-AND-WHY.md](DIFFERENTIATORS-AND-WHY.md) and [PRIVACY-UPGRADES.md](PRIVACY-UPGRADES.md), the owner's 2026-10-20 08:25 ET addition (one root identity usable on any site), and the 0.2.1 code.
- **Lens:** fan-out, churn, ordering, offline delivery, ledger growth, browser-created torrents, phone memory and battery, Service Worker download path, and every number asserted without a measurement.
- **Method:** both proposals read in full; R8 read for its sources; code read in `src/loader/config.js`, `src/loader/torrent.js`, `src/loader/memory-chunk-store.js`, `src/loader/blob-chunk-store.js`, `src/loader/bridge.js`, `src/chain/block.js`, `src/chain/dag.js`, `src/sw/core.js`, `tools/pin.mjs`, and ARCHITECTURE 5.5, 6, 7.1, 8. Block and message sizes in section 1 were **measured** by running the project's own `createBlock`, `blockBytes`, `redact` and `canonicalize` (Node 24.22.0, a throwaway script in the session scratchpad, nothing in the repo changed). Statements marked "from knowledge, verify" are not sourced and carry no weight on their own. No browser was launched; nothing was pushed.
- **Wording:** "no backend", "site ledger"; no em dashes.

## Verdict in one paragraph

Neither proposal can ship as written. One defect blocks shipping: the DM delivery path floods frames with a "hop limit 4" that the frame format has no field for and that relays cannot check for work. That gives every peer an unauthenticated amplifier, and once a hop field is added, dedupe by frame hash breaks. The other findings are major. Holder peers store every flooded envelope for 7 days, which contradicts "live only by default". Presence reaches only direct neighbours, so "who is online" is mostly wrong. "Held by N peers" has no protocol message. Group rekeys fork when two happen at once. Phones cannot seed their own uploads under the existing choke lock. Downloads sit whole in memory because the only writable chunk store is `MemoryChunkStore`, and WebCrypto cannot hash in a stream. The Service Worker has no route for files. Ledger growth from identity and chat-key records fills the 8 MiB default and then silently stops new members. The peer-challenge `chan` binding uses self-declared peer ids, so it does not stop a relay. The identity design does not address the 08:25 cross-site and server-backed sign-in requirement at all. Every one of these has a concrete fix below.

## 2. Findings

### F1. BLOCKER. The flood relay has no hop field, no relay-checkable cost, and a dedupe key that breaks once a hop field exists (RT 4.2, 5.3, 5.6)

- **Problem.** RT 4.2 says ENV and ROOM frames are relayed with "hop limit 4, dedupe by SHA-256 of the frame". The ENV layout `0x03 | 0x01 | tag(8) | epk(65) | iv(12) | ct` has no hop counter, so a hop limit cannot be implemented. If a counter is added and decremented in place, every hop changes the frame bytes and the SHA-256 dedupe no longer matches, so copies circulate until the counter runs out. In a mesh of degree d that is up to d(d-1)^3 copies per message: 750 at d = 6 (arithmetic; 6 is the desktop rendezvous soft cap, `config.js` line 71). Separately, relays cannot check the stranger stamp. It is bound to the recipient's `ek` (RT 5.6), relays do not know which `ek` a tag addresses, and contact frames carry no stamp at all. So a relay forwards any well-formed garbage.
- **Failure scenario.** A hostile peer on the public chat lane sends unique random ENV frames (1,127 bytes each, see F7) at the 64 KiB/s per-peer relay budget. Every honest desktop forwards them to its other 5 to 8 neighbours. Within a few hops every relay's 64 KiB/s outbound budget is used up by junk, and real DMs queue behind it or get dropped. Cost to the attacker: no hashing at all. The "Decrypt attempts capped at 50 per peer per second" limit does not help, because relays do not decrypt.
- **Fix.** (1) Put a 1-byte hop count in a header that dedupe does not cover. Define `frameId = SHA-256(type | version | tag | epk | iv | ct)`, keep it in a seen-set bounded by count and age (for example 20,000 ids or 10 minutes, whichever comes first; to be measured), and decrement the hop count outside the hashed part. (2) Add a relay stamp that anyone can check: `leadingZeroBits(SHA-256("mw-relay-v1|" + frameId + "|" + day + "|" + nonce)) >= relayBits`, carried in the clear header. A relay forwards only frames with a valid stamp. Use low bits for contacts (for example 10) and the stranger level for first contact, measured on a phone (G-CH-6). (3) Use fair queues per inbound neighbour, so one neighbour cannot take more than 1/k of a relay's outbound budget. (4) Forward toward the recipient instead of flooding: once a recipient has sent WANT-TAGS (or a PRES with a pair tag) through a neighbour, send matching frames only to that neighbour and fall back to flooding only when no route is known. (5) Add a test: one hostile context sending at the cap must not delay a contact DM between two honest contexts by more than a stated bound.

### F2. MAJOR. Holders store every flooded envelope, so "live only by default" and D7's "nothing stored by anyone unless they choose" are false (RT 1 bullet 5, 5.7; DIFFERENTIATORS D7)

- **Problem.** RT 5.7 says holders "hold ENV frames whose tags they do not recognise for up to 7 days". Every DM to a non-neighbour is flooded (RT 5.3 "Delivery"), so every holder within 4 hops keeps a copy of every such DM, including ones already delivered live. The sender is never asked, and holders are never told to drop a delivered message. v0.3 has no forward secrecy (RT 5.3), so these copies are exactly what a later chat-key theft decrypts.
- **Failure scenario.** Alice and Bob chat live on a busy desktop-heavy site with history off, believing the default is ephemeral. Ten opt-in holders each keep their envelopes for 7 days. A month later Bob's laptop is stolen with the chat key in IndexedDB. Anyone who ran a holder and kept the frames (nothing stops a modified holder keeping them longer than 7 days) can now read the conversation.
- **Fix.** (1) Add an `hold` flag to the clear header; the sender sets it only when the recipient is not reachable now (no ACK within T) or the visitor chose "deliver later". Holders store only flagged frames. (2) Add a COLLECTED frame: the recipient proves pickup with `HMAC(K_pair, "collected|" + frameId)` for contacts (or a signature by the inbox key for strangers), and holders delete on it. (3) Change the D7 sentence and RT summary 5 to say "messages to people who are offline may be kept, encrypted, by volunteer peers for up to 7 days". (4) Add an E2E check: a message delivered live is not present in any holder's store.

### F3. MAJOR. Presence goes to direct neighbours only, so "Who is online" is wrong most of the time (RT 4.2 PRES "no (neighbours only)", 5.1, 11 "Who is online")

- **Problem.** Wires come from random tracker offers (numwant 3, `config.js` lines 58 to 61) under caps of 6 to 9 on desktop and 3 to 5 on phones (line 71). A contact is a direct neighbour only by chance.
- **Failure scenario.** A site has 50 visitors on the chat lane; Alice has 6 wires. The chance a given online contact is one of her neighbours is about 6/49, about 12% (arithmetic, uniform random wiring assumed). So "Who is online" shows her contact offline roughly 88% of the time while that contact is online. On a phone with 3 wires it is about 6%.
- **Fix.** Either (a) label the list honestly as "connected to you right now" and drop "online"; or (b) relay PRES under the F1 rules with hop limit 2 and a per-origin rate of one beat per 60 s, then measure what it costs on a phone (G-CH-7); or (c) when presence shows a contact is reachable within 2 hops, open a targeted connection (the P2 direct channel, with its IP warning). Pick one and state it in the 9.2 copy.

### F4. MAJOR. "Held by N peers" has no protocol message, and N is not a durable fact (RT 5.7 "States in the UI", 10 `dm.send` states)

- **Problem.** The frame table (RT 4.2) has WANT-TAGS and HELD, which serve the recipient, and ACK, which is for contacts only. Nothing tells the sender that a holder took a copy. Holders are browser tabs: they evict by quota (64 per tag, 4 MiB) and disappear when the tab closes. Unless held frames are written to IndexedDB and the holder comes back, "up to 7 days" really means "while some holder tab stays open". Strangers never get an ACK (by design), so a first-contact message can only ever show "sending" or a guessed state.
- **Failure scenario.** Alice messages Bob, who is offline. Three holder tabs take the frame and the UI says "held by 3 peers". Within an hour all three tabs close. Bob returns the next day and gets nothing, while Alice's screen still says "held by 3".
- **Fix.** Add a HOLDING frame (holder to the depositing neighbour, routed back like an ACK, carrying `frameId` and the holder's expiry) and show "handed to N peers at 14:02; they may go offline". State that holding needs the holder's tab open, persist held frames in IndexedDB only if the holder opts into "keep holding after I close this tab", and re-offer the frame on the sender's next visit if there is no ACK. For strangers, show "sent, delivery unknown" (no ACK by design). Record delivery rates in G-CH-1 and G-CH-3 before the UI ships any of these strings.

### F5. MAJOR. Duplicates and replays come back after every reload, and nothing defines ordering (RT 5.3 "Replay", 5.7)

- **Problem.** The replay seen-set is kept "for the retention window", but with the default (in memory, no history) it is gone after a reload. Holders never learn about delivery (F2), so every WANT-TAGS for 7 days brings back every held envelope. The signed plaintext has `t` but no receiver-side age limit and no per-sender sequence number. Day-rotated tags have no rule for the day boundary.
- **Failure scenario.** Bob opens the site five times in a week; each time HELD returns the same 20 envelopes and the UI shows them as new messages. A malicious holder replays a 6-day-old "meet at 3" message the following week and it is accepted, because nothing bounds `t`. At 23:59 UTC Alice's clock is 2 minutes ahead, so she tags with tomorrow's day and Bob, who asks only for today's tags, misses the message.
- **Fix.** (1) Keep a compact seen-set even in ephemeral mode: per sender, a high-water `t` plus a 7-day Bloom filter of ids, sealed under `K_vault` (it holds no content). (2) Reject plaintexts whose `t` is outside [now - 7 days - 5 min, now + 5 min]. (3) Add a per-conversation counter `n` inside the signed plaintext; display by (`t`, `n`, `id`) and show "earlier messages may still arrive" when `n` has a gap. (4) Define `day` as UTC days since the epoch, and have recipients ask for day - 1, day and day + 1. (5) Holders delete on COLLECTED (F2).

### F6. MAJOR. Group rekeys fork when two happen at once, and members who are offline at a rekey are silently dropped (RT 5.4)

- **Problem.** `epoch` is an integer bumped by whichever admin removes someone. Two admins removing different members at about the same time both create "epoch + 1" with different secrets and different group swarms. Nothing orders or merges them; RT itself notes that MLS needs an ordering service, and this scheme needs one too. A rekey moves the group to a new swarm id, so a member who misses the invite (offline, no holder) keeps listening on a swarm nobody uses any more.
- **Failure scenario.** In a 20-member group, admin A removes M1 at 10:00:05 and admin B removes M2 at 10:00:07. Half the members get A's epoch 2 and half get B's. Each half sees the other's messages as undecryptable, and M1 still holds B's secret. Carol, offline that day, comes back to an empty group with no error.
- **Fix.** Derive the epoch id from its parent, `epochId = SHA-256(prevEpochId | creator | newSecretCommit)`, and adopt a deterministic tie-break: of concurrent children, the lowest `epochId` wins. The losing admin re-applies their removal on top of the winner, and members accept a removal set that is the union of both. Keep listening on the previous epoch's lane for a grace period (for example 7 days) to receive the rekey invite, send rekey invites with the `hold` flag (F2), and show the admin "N members have not confirmed the new key".

### F7. MAJOR. The 256-byte padding bucket cannot hold any message; real frames are about 2.7 times the stated size (RT 5.3 "Sizes", pad buckets)

- **Problem.** The specified plaintext overhead alone is 270 bytes (measured, section 1), so the 256 bucket is never usable. The smallest real ENV frame is 1 + 1 + 8 + 65 + 12 + 1,024 + 16 = 1,127 bytes, not "about 410". Every byte budget built on 410 (mobile relay of 2 MiB per session, holder quotas, the presence and relay arithmetic in R8 F6) is off by that factor.
- **Failure scenario.** A phone that opted into "Share on this network" relays about 1,860 frames per session at the real size, not the about 5,100 implied by 410-byte frames (arithmetic: 2 MiB / 1,127 B versus 2 MiB / 410 B).
- **Fix.** Either shrink the plaintext (binary encoding: 16-byte id, 8-byte `t`, 1-byte kind, 65-byte raw key, 64-byte raw signature, so 154 bytes of overhead and a usable 256 bucket for bodies up to about 100 bytes), or drop the 256 bucket and restate the sizes as 1,127 / 4,199 / 16,103 bytes on the wire. Add a test vector asserting the frame length for each bucket.

### F8. MAJOR. Chat lanes, group lanes and file torrents multiply WebRTC connections past the B6 phone budget, and none of them has a cap (RT 4.1, 5.4, 6.2; ARCHITECTURE 8 B6)

- **Problem.** B6 defines caps for two swarm kinds, `site` and `rendezvous` (`torrent.js` `_caps`, `config.js` line 71). RT adds a chat lane, one swarm per private group (and a new one on every rekey) and one torrent per shared file, and gives none of them a cap. RT 6.2 says file torrents "have their own caps" without numbers. WebTorrent wires belong to one torrent, so the same remote visitor in the rendezvous swarm and the chat lane is two peer connections, each with its own ICE and DTLS setup and keepalives (from knowledge, verify against WebTorrent 3.0.21). Each extra swarm also adds tracker announces to every tracker in the list, including the 55 s offer-free keepalive (`config.js` line 42). The rejected alternative in RT 4.1 ("every reader would carry chat traffic") is not true: `mw_chat1` would attach only to wires where both sides advertise it, as `mw_chain1` already does (ARCHITECTURE 6.6).
- **Failure scenario.** A phone member with chat on, in 3 groups, fetching 1 file: site (soft 6 on mobileUnknown) + rendezvous (3 to 5) + chat lane + 3 group lanes + 1 file torrent. With 3 to 5 wires per new swarm that is about 24 to 36 peer connections, against a mobileMetered `maxConns` of 4 (`config.js` line 61) (arithmetic). Battery and memory use grow with each one, and B6's scoring (a `mw_chain1` wire scores highest) has no rule for wires that speak only `mw_chat1`.
- **Fix.** (1) Default: register `mw_chat1` on rendezvous wires whose handshake advertises it; join the separate chat lane only when fewer than k (for example 2) rendezvous neighbours have chat on. (2) Carry group traffic on the chat lane with a per-group tag and join a group's own swarm only while that group's view is open. (3) Add one budget across all swarms per policy class (for example desktop 40, phones 8 wires in total), with priority site > rendezvous > chat > open group > files, and B6 scoring rules for `mw_chat1` wires. (4) Leave a file torrent once the file is saved, unless the visitor chose to keep sharing. Measure connection counts and battery on G-CH-2 and G-CH-7.

### F9. MAJOR. Phones cannot seed their own uploads under the existing choke lock, and a short app switch tears down chat and transfers (RT 6.5, 7, 9.3; ARCHITECTURE 8)

- **Problem.** On every mobile class, uploads are off by default, enforced as a choke lock on every wire of every torrent (`torrent.js` `_lockAll`, `setHiddenLock` lines 773 to 783; `config.js` lines 59 to 61). RT 6.5 tells a phone uploader "This file is available only while you keep this page open", but with the default policy nobody can download a single piece from that phone. A hidden phone page is choke-locked at once and the client is rebuilt after 10 s hidden (`config.js` line 66). That rebuild drops the chat lane, presence, held frames in memory and any file torrent in flight.
- **Failure scenario.** G-CH-4 as written: an Android phone shares a 25 MiB file with an iPhone. The recipient connects and requests pieces, the sender stays choked, and the transfer stalls at 0%. If the visitor opts in, the cap is 128 KiB/s (`config.js` line 62), so 25 MiB takes at least 200 s with the page open and visible (arithmetic). Switching to a messaging app for 15 s to tell the recipient "check now" chokes the upload and, after 10 s, rebuilds the client, so the transfer starts over.
- **Fix.** Add a per-torrent upload exception for a visitor's own upload, with its own card ("Sending this file uses your mobile data; keep this page open and on screen until it says sent"), not tied to "Share on this network". Show "sent: one full copy reached another visitor" only after a peer has every piece. Keep file torrents' stores across `rebuild()` the way the site torrent's `RetainedStore` already is (`torrent.js` lines 400 to 436). Say plainly that leaving the page for more than 10 s on a phone pauses chat and transfers. If the owner has a pin node, route phone uploads to it first.

### F10. MAJOR. Identity, chat-key and record writes fill the 8 MiB default ledger, after which new members silently cannot chat (ID 6.2, 9 "Ledger bloat"; RT 5.2; ARCHITECTURE 6.3)

- **Problem.** The ID threat row says "updates tombstone the old", which suggests the space comes back. It does not. A tombstone frees only the value and salt; the old stub (about 409 bytes accounted) stays, and the tombstone block itself adds 383 to 852 bytes (measured, section 1). The net cost of a record update is about +792 bytes at prev 1 (1,877 new + 383 tombstone + 409 old stub, minus the 1,877 before), and the record itself costs 1,877. Each `mw.ck` rotation adds 636. Beyond `maxChainBytes`, only owner, moderator and allow-listed blocks are ingested (ARCHITECTURE 6.3).
- **Failure scenario.** An open site with the default 8 MiB cap: each member who signs in, migrates, enables chat and syncs a record costs about 636 + 735 + 1,877 = 3,248 bytes; with 10 record updates and 2 key rotations, about 3,248 + 7,920 + 1,272 = 12,440 bytes. That reaches 8 MiB at about 670 members, with no guestbook posts counted (arithmetic from measured sizes). After that, a new member's `mw.ck` is refused by every peer, so writers get `E_NO_CHAT_KEY` for that member, and record updates fail for everyone, with no message saying why. The `cs=` snapshot every new visitor downloads grows by the same amount.
- **Fix.** (1) Put the measured per-member budget in ID 9 and RT 7, and add a "ledger capacity" line to the owner's tools (members times bytes). (2) Store `ek` inside the existing `profile` value (one LWW slot per author, so a rotation replaces it instead of piling up). RT rejected this for v0.3, but the growth argument is a reason to reconsider. (3) Rate-limit sealed-record syncs (for example at most one synced update per hour; local writes are unlimited). (4) Above 80% of the cap, have the loader and SDK report `E_LEDGER_FULL` instead of a silent refusal. (5) Make `checkpoint` (reserved) the path for pruning stubs, and say that until it exists, growth is monotonic.

### F11. MAJOR. Downloads are held whole in memory, and the plaintext SHA-256 cannot be computed as a stream with WebCrypto (RT 6.2, 6.3, 10 `files.fetch`, 12 E-FI-4)

- **Problem.** RT 6.2 says pieces go to "the existing blob chunk store; nothing is assembled whole in memory on a phone". `BlobChunkStore` is read-only: it serves an existing cached site (`blob-chunk-store.js` lines 1 to 6). Downloads use `MemoryChunkStore` (`torrent.js` lines 1541 to 1544), which is RAM. `files.fetch` returns a whole `blob`, and decryption produces a second copy. The envelope carries `sha256 (plaintext)`, but `SubtleCrypto.digest` takes the whole buffer and has no incremental mode, so checking it needs the whole plaintext in one ArrayBuffer.
- **Failure scenario.** A desktop fetches a 100 MiB private file: about 100 MiB of ciphertext pieces in `MemoryChunkStore`, a 100 MiB plaintext Blob, and a 100 MiB ArrayBuffer for the digest, so roughly 300 MiB at the peak (arithmetic). On a phone at the 25 MiB cap, while the 48 MiB site is also in memory, iOS may kill the tab (from knowledge, verify on G-CH-4). E-FI-4 cannot pass as written.
- **Fix.** (1) Drop the plaintext `sha256` for private files: STREAM-style AES-GCM records with a final flag already authenticate every record and the truncation point. For public files, rely on the torrent's piece hashes and compute SHA-256 with the repo's incremental JS `sha256-sync.js` in a worker if it is kept. (2) Build a writable IndexedDB chunk store for file torrents (not the default `fsa-chunk-store`, which `memory-chunk-store.js` lines 1 to 3 explain is unsafe) and decrypt record by record into an IndexedDB-backed save path. (3) Change `files.fetch` to return a handle and a `ReadableStream`, not a whole Blob. (4) Until (2) exists, cap private files at the phone limit on every class and say "files are held in memory while open".

### F12. MAJOR. The Service Worker has no route, data source or deadline rule for file downloads (RT 6.6, 10 `files.save`; ARCHITECTURE 7.1, 7.4)

- **Problem.** RT 6.6 says uploaded bytes "are delivered by the Service Worker with `Content-Disposition: attachment`". Today the worker serves only verified site files from IndexedDB, keyed by btih (ARCHITECTURE 7.1). File bytes live in the page (F11), not the worker. In shell mode every top-level navigation is answered with the precached `/index.html`, so a download link that navigates is swallowed unless a route goes first. Every navigation must also be answered within 25 s (`navigationDeadlineMs`), while a file may still be downloading. Degraded mode (no worker, 7.4) and drop-in mode, where navigations go to the network (3.4.2 a), get no path at all.
- **Failure scenario.** A shell-mode visitor clicks "Save" on a 60 MiB file that is 40% fetched. The navigation gets `index.html` (the site reloads inside itself), or after 25 s the "Still loading" interstitial. A drop-in visitor's click goes to the host, which returns a 404.
- **Fix.** Specify a reserved path (for example `/_mw/f/<ref>`) routed before the navigation catch-all, answered only for a ref whose bytes are complete and stored in IndexedDB (F11), with `Content-Disposition: attachment`, `nosniff`, `Content-Type: application/octet-stream`, `Cache-Control: no-store` and Range support as for site files. Enable "Save" only when the file is complete. In degraded and drop-in modes, fall back to a Blob URL with `a[download]`, and add a real-device row for iOS Safari attachment behaviour.

### F13. MAJOR. WANT-TAGS links the recipient's IP to the sender's IP at a holder, so the exposure table understates what holders learn (RT 5.7, 9.1 "Holder peer", "Owner pin node mailbox")

- **Problem.** RT 5.7 says holders learn "never sender or recipient". At the IP level they learn both. The holder sees the IP of the neighbour that deposited a tag (often the sender), and later the IP that asks for that tag in WANT-TAGS (the recipient). A pin node mailbox, which talks to everyone, sees this for every pair, every day. Separately, the stranger inbox tag `SHA-256("mw-inbox-v1|" + ek + "|" + day)` can be computed by anyone. So anyone can ask for a member's inbox and learn when that member is online and how much stranger mail they get. And at about 2^20 hashes per stamp, an attacker can fill the 64-per-tag quota and evict real first-contact messages, since eviction is oldest first.
- **Failure scenario.** A site owner runs the pin node as a mailbox. After a week it holds a per-day table of (depositor IP, tag, collector IP), which is who writes to whom, by IP. The 9.1 row "as holder plus IP" does not say this.
- **Fix.** Rewrite the holder and pin-node rows as "can link the IP that left a message to the IP that collected it, per day". Mitigations: recipients ask for their real tags mixed with decoy tags drawn from the same distribution, collect through a different neighbour than the one they are wired to most, and collect after a random delay. Holders evict per tag by stamp strength rather than age. For the inbox tag, require the collector to prove possession of the chat key: the holder sends a random challenge and the collector answers with `ECDH(ek_holder_ephemeral, ek)` before HELD is sent.

### F14. MAJOR. The peer challenge's `chan` binding uses self-declared peer ids, so it does not stop a relay (ID 4.1 "Challenge-response", 9 "Replay of a peer challenge")

- **Problem.** The BitTorrent peer id is chosen by each side and sent in its handshake, unauthenticated. A relay M between verifier A and prover B can tell B that its id is A's and tell A that its id is B's. Both sides then compute the same `chan: [idA, idB]`, and B's signed proof checks out at A. ID 9 lists `chan` as the mitigation and only DTLS binding as open.
- **Failure scenario.** M joins the chat lane, connects to A and B, and forwards A's nonce to B under A's peer id. B signs; A accepts M's session as "B signed in" for "chat sessions, file-share accept". Anything gated on that session rather than on B's keys is now M's.
- **Fix.** Bind the proof to a key, not to a wire label. The verifier sends `{ nonce, epk_A }`, and the prover's proof covers `SHA-256(epk_A | epk_B)`, with every later frame in the session encrypted under `ECDH(epk_A, epk_B)`. A relay without B's key cannot then use the session. Better still, gate nothing on peer sessions: every RT action is already signed per message and encrypted to `ek`. Then delete the `chan` row from ID 9 and say peer login proofs only display who signed.

### F15. MAJOR. The 08:25 requirement (one root identity on any site, including server-backed ones) is not designed, and the parts it needs are distributed-state problems (ID 2 non-goals, 3.1, 4.1, 7)

- **Problem.** ID lists "cross-site identity by default" as a non-goal and rejects SIWE, but says nothing about (a) the drop-in widget as a sign-in for existing sites, (b) a published challenge format for server-backed sites, (c) a Node and browser verification library, or (d) the visible "use the same identity here" choice. The parts that exist do not carry over. `verifyLogin` checks `c.aud === location.origin`, which a Node verifier does not have. Derivation is keyed on `ledgerId` (`info = "seed|" + ledgerId`), which a server-backed site does not have. Self-issued bearer proofs carry a loader nonce and live up to 7 days, so a server cannot tell a fresh proof from a replayed one.
- **Failure scenario.** A server-backed forum accepts magnet-web sign-in proofs. An attacker who copies one bearer proof from a visitor's tab (an XSS on the forum, or a proxy log) replays it to the forum's API for 7 days. Two servers behind a load balancer each accept the same challenge once, because each only remembers its own nonces. A phone 3 minutes slow fails `iat - 2 min <= now`.
- **Fix.** Add a section to ID that defines: a derivation for origins without a ledger (`info = "seed|origin|" + registrableDomain`, with mirrors handled through Related Origin Requests, as passkeys are); a server challenge `{ domain, uri, ledger | origin, nonce (server-issued, 128 bits), iat, exp <= 10 min, statement, chain: 'mw-p256' }`; a verification library `verifyServerLogin(proof, { expectedDomain, expectedOrigin, consumeNonce })`, where `consumeNonce` is an atomic, single-use check in the server's shared store (documented as required, with a reference in-memory version for one process only); a skew allowance of ±5 min configured at the verifier; bearer proofs never accepted by servers; and the "use the same identity here" link as a signed `mw.link` statement between two site keys, shown in a loader card. Add vectors shared by Node and both browser engines, and a test that a replayed proof fails at a second server instance sharing the store.

### F16. MAJOR. The owner pin node, the only lasting copy of a file, keeps files in a Chromium tab's memory, and there is no web seed fallback (RT 6.1 F4, 6.5, 5.7; `tools/pin.mjs` lines 2 to 5)

- **Problem.** `pin.mjs` is the real loader running in Playwright Chromium. File torrents there would use the same `MemoryChunkStore` (F11), so pinning 256 MiB of public files means 256 MiB of tab memory, and a crash or restart (which the tool does with backoff) loses every pinned file until peers bring it back, and by then they may be gone. RT gives the pin node "larger quotas" as a mailbox without saying where they are stored. RT never covers a web seed for user files. Public files are plaintext and private files are ciphertext, so an owner-run HTTPS copy (BEP 19 `url-list`) is safe for both and gives the only durable fallback that involves no backend: a static upload done by the owner.
- **Failure scenario.** The owner pins 30 public files from an event. The pin node restarts overnight. The next morning, visitors' fetches stall with `E_FILE_UNAVAILABLE`, because the attendees who had the files closed their tabs.
- **Fix.** Pin node: persist pinned file torrents to disk (the profile directory) through the IndexedDB chunk store of F11, or add a Node-side seeder in `tools/seed.mjs` that writes `.torrent` plus data to disk. Add an optional `ws` (web seed URL) field to `mw.files` listings and the private-share envelope, filled in when the owner copies the file to static hosting with a `tools/` command, served under the 6.6 attachment rule and never at a path the site's own code can import. State in RT 6.5 that without a pin node on disk or a web seed, files have no durability.

### F17. MINOR. Image previews can be decompression bombs (RT 6.6)

- **Problem and scenario.** `createImageBitmap` decodes the whole image. A 30 KiB PNG that declares 20,000 by 20,000 pixels needs about 1.6 GB as RGBA (arithmetic: 20,000 x 20,000 x 4), which can crash the tab on a phone.
- **Fix.** Parse the dimensions from the PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP header before decoding, refuse previews above a pixel cap (for example 16 megapixels), and decode in a worker with `resizeWidth` and `resizeHeight`.

### F18. MINOR. Public listing sizes are self-declared, so the 256 MiB per-site cap is not enforceable as specified (RT 6.4, 7)

- **Problem and scenario.** `len` in `mw.files` is whatever the author writes. An author lists a 2 GiB torrent as `len: 1000`, the cap sum stays low, and fetchers start a 2 GiB download.
- **Fix.** After metadata arrives, compare the torrent's real length with `len`, abort and hide the listing on a mismatch, and count only verified lengths toward the per-site cap. Abort any fetch above the per-file cap whatever `len` says.

### F19. MINOR. File storage on phones has no budget or eviction rule, and received file keys are lost when ITP clears storage (RT 6.2, 7; ID 6.2 fields)

- **Problem and scenario.** The phone storage budget is 128 MiB per browser (`config.js` line 30) and the site cap is 48 MiB. Two kept 25 MiB files, the site, a 2 to 8 MiB ledger and history together are close to the budget, and LRU never evicts the current site, so eviction falls on other sites. The record's `files` field holds keys of the visitor's own uploads only. Keys of files received from others live only in the local vault, which ITP can delete after 7 days without a visit (ID 3.5).
- **Fix.** Add a files line inside the storage budget (for example at most 50% of what is left after the site and ledger), LRU across files, and delete a received file's store after "Save". Add received file keys to the synced record (they fit: 32 + 8 bytes each) or tell the visitor that saving the file is the only way to keep it.

### F20. MINOR. Sending while the ledger is still syncing reports "no chat key" (RT 10 `E_NO_CHAT_KEY`; ARCHITECTURE 5.5)

- **Problem and scenario.** The site renders before the ledger syncs and signatures verify in a pool of 2 (`config.js` line 95). A new visitor who sends a DM right after sign-in gets `E_NO_CHAT_KEY` for a member whose `mw.ck` simply has not arrived yet, and the UI tells them that member cannot be messaged.
- **Fix.** Add `E_SYNCING` (the ledger is not yet at its peers' `root`), and retry the lookup when `node.subscribe` fires. Also say how long a full sync takes at 8 MiB on a phone (to be measured).

## 3. What was checked and found sound

- The 16 KiB frame cap under the 262,144-byte wire guard: the largest bucket (16,103 bytes on the wire with header and tag) plus the 6-byte BEP 10 framing fits.
- `maxBlockBytes` 4,096 holds the sealed record even at 8 parents (2,346 bytes accounted, 2,528 on the wire, measured).
- A 25 to 100 MiB file at about 1,024 pieces stays under the wire guard for metadata and piece messages: about 20 KiB of piece hashes (arithmetic, create-torrent default per R8 F1).
- Keeping conversation out of the ledger (RT 3) is the right call for growth reasons as well as privacy reasons: see the per-block sizes in section 1.

## 4. Not verified in this pass

- WebTorrent 3.0.21 connection sharing across torrents (F8) is from knowledge and must be confirmed in source.
- iOS memory limits for a tab (F11) and Safari's behaviour with attachment responses from a Service Worker (F12) need the real-device rows.
- No code was run in a browser; the sizes in section 1 come from Node only. WebCrypto output sizes are the same in browsers, but this pass did not confirm that.
