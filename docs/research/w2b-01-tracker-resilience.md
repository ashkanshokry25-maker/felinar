## Summary

Scope: WebSocket tracker resilience and self-hosting for magnet-web's production wave. Everything marked "measured" was run on 2026-10-17 and is reproducible from `labs/w2b-01-tracker-resilience-ref/`; numbers have a ±25% host-noise caveat where stated.

1. **The public set is the same four trackers and it held:** `tracker.webtorrent.dev`, `tracker.openwebtorrent.com`, `open.ftorrent.com`, `tracker.novage.com.ua` - 17 samples over 1.4 hours (16 counted; 1 excluded as a local fault): webtorrent.dev 16/16, openwebtorrent 16/16, ftorrent 16/16, novage 16/16; offer->answer relay tests 24/24 passed. `tracker.btorrent.xyz` (A = 127.0.0.1) and `tracker.files.fm:7073` (403 for foreign origins) are still dead/locked, and the project's placeholder `CONFIG.trackers` still lists both. No operator publishes an SLA or abuse policy (F1).
2. **Ad-blockers: the tracker hostnames are not in EasyList, EasyPrivacy, uBlock (default set), Brave's default bundle, AdGuard Base/Tracking or Firefox's Disconnect list - but DNS block lists block them:** HaGeZi Anti-Piracy blocks all four public trackers, BlockListProject "torrent" blocks two of them (openwebtorrent, novage) plus `files.fm` and `webtorrent.io`, HaGeZi Light/Pro/Ultimate block the MQTT brokers `broker.emqx.io` and `broker.hivemq.com`, BlockListProject "tracking" blocks `stun.l.google.com`, EasyPrivacy blocks third-party WebSockets to `duckdns.org`. A tracker on a neutral hostname under our own domain is the one entry nobody lists (F2; closes review item m8).
3. **The stock `bittorrent-tracker` 11.2.3 server dies on one malformed message** (`offers:[null]` -> uncaught `TypeError`, exit 1; PeerTube's server-only fork is byte-identical) and is unbounded in other ways (no ping, 100 MiB frames, unlimited swarms/backlog, `/stats` blocks the loop). A 416-line wrapper (`tracker/tracker.mjs`) fixes that: 16 black-box tests pass on macOS and on Ubuntu 24.04.5, the systemd unit scores 3.3 "OK" in `systemd-analyze security` (F3).
4. **Capacity is not the constraint:** measured about 9 KiB retained (19 KiB RSS) per idle socket for the stock server and 12 KiB (25 KiB RSS) with the wrapper; one core saturates near 15-23k announces/s (each fanning out 5 offers), i.e. above 1 M sockets at the real 120 s cadence; 20,000 sockets need about 0.5 GB. Live operators: 288k sockets reported by a single Node process's stats (openwebtorrent, 2.78 GB RSS), 120k on two Ampere cores (novage, Rust). The expected small-site load is 10-1,400 concurrent sockets (F3).
5. **Cost:** Oracle Always Free A1 $0 (10 TB egress, idle-reclaim and capacity caveats); a DigitalOcean $4-6 droplet, Fly.io $2.19-6.70 (+$0.02/GB), Hetzner EU with ≥ 20 TB included (prices not retrievable here); Cloudflare Worker + Durable Object about $5 up to ~2.5 M visits/month, $8.5 at 10 M, linear after that (F4, F6).
6. **Caddy 2.11.7 in front is a 6-line config and behaves:** WebSockets pass, spoofed `X-Forwarded-For` is ignored, the server idle timeout does not cut upgraded sockets, but **`caddy reload` closes every socket in 71 ms** (8,049 ms with `stream_close_delay 8s`) and the nested `servers { metrics }` option is deprecated (F5).
7. **Cloudflare Durable Objects can host a WebTorrent tracker:** a 110-line prototype passes 8/8 checks, survives hibernation (state in socket attachments), and two real WebTorrent pages connected through it in 160 ms (local `workerd`); one socket = one object, so the swarm goes into the URL; deploys disconnect everyone (F6).
8. **The client, not the tracker, is where resilience is won or lost:** after a 2-second tracker blip the stock client needs a median **174 s (max 306 s)** to come back; a per-instance reconnect override brings 12 of 12 back in **1-2 s**. The stock client creates 5 `RTCPeerConnection`s per tracker per cycle even in an empty swarm (45 in 30 s for 3 trackers at a 10 s scaled interval); a count-aware probe-then-offer policy creates **0** while alone or connected, cuts tracker bytes by 95% and adds 16 ms to the first connection (F7).
9. **Graceful degradation works only with `xs=` and `ws=`:** with both, the site loads in 18-85 ms (loopback) under dead, black-hole, mute, alive-but-empty, NXDOMAIN or absent trackers in Chromium and WebKit; without `xs` nothing loads; `xs` without `ws` stalls after metadata (F10).
10. **Tracker-less fallbacks:** 11 of 12 Nostr relays pass an ephemeral-event round trip (none on any block list; NIP-66 monitors corroborate), 4 of 4 MQTT brokers pass but two are DNS-blocked and all are "testing" infrastructure; Trystero 0.26.0 is current and has changed its default relay lists about five times in 20 months (F8).
11. **Monitoring:** none of the UptimeRobot (free: 50 monitors @ 5 min) or Better Stack (free: 10 monitors/heartbeats) pages lists a WebSocket monitor; use HTTP/keyword + TCP checks, a heartbeat fed by `probes/check_trackers.py` (announce + offer relay) run from another host or GitHub Actions, Prometheus rules (11, validated with `promtool`), and a daily block-list job (F9).
12. **Recommended v1 signalling:** `tr=` = our neutral-host tracker(s) first, then the four public ones; `ws=` + `xs=` always; client shim for reconnect/keepalive; count-aware announce policy; Nostr room for the chain; web seed as the floor. Fifteen actions with priority and effort are in "Concrete recommendations".

### F1. The live public set on 2026-10-17 is the same four trackers, and none failed on its own account in a 2-hour soak `[DESIGN-CRITICAL]`

**Claim.** `wss://tracker.webtorrent.dev`, `wss://tracker.openwebtorrent.com`, `wss://open.ftorrent.com` and `wss://tracker.novage.com.ua` are the only general-purpose public WebSocket trackers that pass a full signalling test; three days after the first wave nothing changed, and across the soak window below no sample of any of them failed except one (21:30:06Z) where three of them timed out together during my own local load test - excluded below with the reason, raw data untouched. ### F2. Ad-blockers, privacy lists and DNS blocklists: the public trackers are NOT in browser lists, but DNS anti-piracy/torrent lists block them (all four in HaGeZi Anti-Piracy) and two MQTT brokers sit on mainstream DNS lists `[DESIGN-CRITICAL]`

Closes review item `docs/review/browser-mobile.md` m8 ("grep each hostname against EasyList, EasyPrivacy and Brave's lists").

**Claim 1 (browser-level blockers: clean).** On 2026-10-17 none of the browser ad-block lists that Brave Shields, uBlock Origin, AdGuard and EasyList-based blockers ship by default blocks any of: `wss://tracker.webtorrent.dev`, `wss://tracker.openwebtorrent.com`, `wss://open.ftorrent.com`, `wss://tracker.novage.com.ua`, the dead `tracker.btorrent.xyz`, `tracker.files.fm:7073`, six Nostr relays (nos.lol, relay.damus.io, relay.primal.net, ...), the four Trystero MQTT brokers, `cloudflare-dns.com/dns-query` and `dns.google/resolve` (the DoH pointer lookups), or three invented neutral names (`rtc.`, `signal.`, `t.example-site.org`). **What this means for design.**
1. Browser-level blocking is *not* a reason to avoid the public trackers today; DNS-level "piracy/torrent" categories are. Visitors behind such filters (parental-control DNS, some schools and offices) lose *every* public tracker at once, and the failure is silent to JavaScript (a refused/NXDOMAIN WebSocket shows only `onerror` + close 1006).
2. The **MQTT fallback is a weaker "independent second plane" than F8 of the earlier note assumed**: two of its four default brokers are in mainstream default-recommended DNS lists. Nostr relays are not.
3. A self-hosted tracker on a **neutral hostname under our own domain** (`rtc.<domain>`, `sig.<domain>`) avoids hostname-keyword and "known tracker" listings. Do not use the strings `tracker`, `torrent`, `peer`, `bt`, or free dynamic-DNS suffixes such as `duckdns.org`.
4. Treat tracker reachability as a measurable property: ship a daily CI job that re-runs the list check for every hostname in `config.js` (scripts: `labs/w2b-01-tracker-resilience-ref/probes/filterlist-check/`).
5. Corporate web gateways classify P2P and file-sharing hosts by category (vendor databases such as Cisco Umbrella, Zscaler, Palo Alto URL filtering). ### F3. Self-hosting engines: capacity is not the constraint for our workload; the stock Node server is crashable and unbounded, and a ~400-line wrapper fixes that `[DESIGN-CRITICAL]`

**Claim 1 - the stock `bittorrent-tracker` 11.2.3 WebSocket server can be crashed by one unauthenticated message.** Reproduced on 2026-10-17 (`tracker/test/stock-crash-demo.mjs`): a client that sends `{"action":"announce", ..., "offers":[null]}` into a swarm that already contains one other peer makes `server.js:528` (`params.offers[i].offer`) throw `TypeError: Cannot read properties of null (reading 'offer')` inside a `process.nextTick` callback; there is no `try/catch` on that path, so the Node process exits with code 1; reproduced against the documented command itself, `bittorrent-tracker --ws --port N` (`tracker/test/stock-cli-crash-demo.mjs`: exit code 1, `server.js:528`), because the bundled CLI has no `uncaughtException` handler. ### F6. A WebTorrent tracker on Cloudflare Workers + Durable Objects: it works (prototype passes with real WebTorrent clients), costs about the $5 plan minimum, and has three design constraints `[DESIGN-CRITICAL for the "no server we run" option]`

**Claim.** A Durable Object (DO) per swarm using the WebSocket Hibernation API is a working WebTorrent-protocol tracker. I built a ~110-line prototype (`labs/w2b-01-tracker-resilience-ref/cf-do/`) and ran it in `workerd` 2026-10-12 via `wrangler dev --local` 4.151.1:
- 8/8 black-box checks pass (announce reply with `interval: 120`; offer relayed to a random other peer; answer relayed back; `offers:[null]` rejected without a crash; `peer_id` changing on a connection rejected; wrong swarm rejected; bad path -> HTTP 400; clean close handshake).
- **Hibernation-safe:** with 25 s of silence the object was evicted and its constructor re-ran on the next message ("Swarm constructed" logged twice) and the offer was still relayed in 22 ms, because all peer state lives in per-socket attachments (`serializeAttachment`, 16,384-byte limit) rather than in memory.
- **Real clients:** two headless Chromium pages running the project's `webtorrent.min.js` 3.0.21: page A filled itself from the HTTP web seed, page B had *no* web seed and *no* `xs=`, only `tr=ws://127.0.0.1:8787/<btih>`; B announced at 140 ms, got a WebRTC wire at 151 ms, metadata at 153 ms and completed the 473,645-byte torrent from A at 184 ms (`do-e2e.mjs`). So the stock client speaks to it without modification.
Not tested: Cloudflare's production edge (latency, real hibernation timing, deploy-time disconnects), billing, abuse at scale.

**Existing implementations (as of 2026-10-17):** only one public repo implements the WebTorrent WS announce/offer/answer messages on DOs: `manalejandro/cf-feditorrent` (MIT, 0 stars, 10 commits; the tracker is a component of an ActivityPub torrent-sharing app; uses `acceptWebSocket` + `serializeAttachment`; authorises info hashes through a KV lookup; no rate limiting, no peer caps, answers accepted from unannounced senders, a malformed bencode failure string, offers silently dropped when the swarm is small). The earlier note (F17: "none implements...") was wrong on that point; it is a toy, not a drop-in. `yanshiqwq/cf-http-tracker` (HTTP announce only) and `99percentpeople/weblink-ws-worker` (generic room signalling) were seen in search hits only and not read. GitHub topic `webtorrent-tracker` has no repositories.

**Design constraints.**
1. **One socket = one Durable Object.** The stock client multiplexes all torrents of one tracker URL over one socket, so the swarm must be in the URL: `wss://<worker>/<40-hex btih>`. The loader controls `tr=`, so that is free, and it isolates each site's swarm and bounds blast radius. The URL is also a natural place for a *capability token* (`?k=<HMAC(secret, btih)>`, produced by `publish.mjs`) so strangers cannot create unlimited DOs on our bill (each new room = billed requests); verify it in the Worker before `idFromName`.
2. **Deploys disconnect every WebSocket** (Cloudflare docs: code deploys and restarts terminate sockets); the loader's reconnect logic (R4) must therefore be fast and jittered, not the stock 10 s + U(0, 5 min).
3. **In-memory state resets on hibernation**: keep peer state in attachments (done) and treat `getWebSockets()` (O(n) per announce) as the peer table; fine for swarms of hundreds, re-think above ~1,000 peers per swarm.

### F7. Multi-tracker redundancy and announce tuning: the interval is not the lever; offers per announce, the first announce and reconnect behaviour are `[DESIGN-CRITICAL]`

**How a small swarm really forms (code-verified, `bittorrent-tracker` 11.2.3 client + `torrent-discovery` + `webtorrent` 3.0.21).**
1. **Discovery is triggered by the later arrival.** A tracker only relays offers; the first visitor sits on its tracker socket and answers whatever is relayed. The joiner's first announce carries the offers, so a pair connects about one tracker round trip plus ICE after the second visitor arrives, **independent of the announce interval** (measured below: 142-161 ms to the first WebRTC wire on loopback under every policy and a 10 s interval). The interval only matters for healing (replacing lost connections), for tracker-side liveness (not needed: the tracker drops a peer when its socket closes) and for mobile resume.
2. **Offers are the cost.** `WebSocketTracker.announce` creates `min(numwant, 5)` fresh `RTCPeerConnection`s *per tracker per announce* (default `numwant` 50); each lives up to 50 s (`OFFER_TIMEOUT`). `torrent-discovery` and the client re-announce on the server's `interval` (120 s on three public trackers, 180 s on novage). With T trackers the stock client therefore creates **5T connections per cycle even in an empty swarm**: measured 45 in 30 s for T = 3 at a 10 s interval, i.e. **15 per 2-minute cycle, 450 per hour, per tab**, almost all of them wasted when nobody is there (the tracker discards offers that have no recipient).
3. **`getAnnounceOpts` is evaluated on every announce and overrides `numwant`** (`client.js _defaultAnnounceOpts`; WebTorrent merges `client.tracker.getAnnounceOpts` and the per-torrent option at call time), and `numwant: 0` produces an announce with no offers and no `RTCPeerConnection` (verified: P4 below). Tracker responses carry the swarm size (`complete`, `incomplete`), exposed as `torrent.discovery.tracker.on('update', ...)`.
4. **Peers meet only through a tracker both can reach**: with reachable sets S1, S2 the pair is introduced iff S1 and S2 intersect. That is why every peer must announce to **all** listed trackers and why one tracker nobody blocks (a neutral hostname we control, F2) is worth more than a fifth public one: a visitor behind a DNS "piracy" list sees only that tracker, and still meets every other visitor.
5. **Reconnect is slow (measured).** After a tracker dies the stock client waits `floor(random() * 300000) + min(2^retries * 10 s, 1 h)` and `announce()` is a no-op while it waits. 12 independent clients, tracker killed for **2 seconds** and restarted on the same port - seconds until each was back:

- **stock WebTorrent 3.0.21 / bittorrent-tracker 11.2.3**: 12 clients, tracker killed (SIGKILL) for 2 s and restarted on the same port; seconds until each client was back on the tracker: 15, 15, 39, 77, 79, 165, 174, 223, 233, 251, 280, 306; median 174 s, max 306 s, never back within the window: 0.
- **per-instance `_startReconnectTimer` override (500 ms + U(0, min(60 s, 1 s x 2^retries)))**: 12 clients, tracker killed (SIGKILL) for 2 s and restarted on the same port; seconds until each client was back on the tracker: 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2; median 1 s, max 2 s, never back within the window: 0.

   A 2-second blip (a Caddy reload, a deploy, a Cloudflare restart) removes a visitor from the swarm for **3 minutes on average and up to 5**; the override above (`tracker._startReconnectTimer`, reachable as `torrent.discovery.tracker._trackers[i]`, an internal API: pin `webtorrent@3.0.21` and keep this experiment as the test) brings everybody back in **1-2 s**.
6. **Idle closes are shorter than the interval** (first-wave F10: 125-184 s on the live public trackers against a 120 s announce interval; re-measured 2026-10-17 with the same 240 s silent-socket test, `probes/results/idle-test-2026-10-17.json`: closed by the peer after **125 s** (openwebtorrent), **131 s** (webtorrent.dev) and **188 s** (ftorrent), novage still open at 245 s with its 180 s interval - unchanged after three days). Our own tracker pings every 30 s, which keeps proxies and NATs alive and kills dead peers without any client code (browsers answer WebSocket pings in their network stack without running page JavaScript - expected, **not tested** under background throttling; Chrome's intensive timer throttling - hidden > 5 min, chain count >= 5, silent 30 s - exempts only pages with an open `RTCDataChannel`, so a lone visitor's timers are throttled to one run per minute). For the public trackers the only lever is a shorter client cadence: cap each tracker object's `setInterval` at about 55 s **and make those extra announces `numwant: 0`**.

**Policy experiment (real WebTorrent 3.0.21 bundle in Chromium 156, 3 local reference trackers, announce interval scaled to 10 s, one lone page for 30 s, then a second page joins, then 30 s steady state; `e2e/tuning.mjs`):**

| Policy (3 trackers, announce interval scaled to 10 s; x12 for the real 120 s) | RTCPeerConnections, page alone, 30 s | Bytes sent to trackers, alone, 30 s | Time for a joiner's first WebRTC wire | RTCPeerConnections per page, 2 peers connected, 30 s | Tracker bytes sent / received by page A, connected, 30 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 stock: numwant 50 capped to 5 offers per tracker per announce | 45 | 31076 | 145 ms | 54 (A) / 54 (B) | 31005 / 7776 |
| P1 fixed numwant 2 | 18 | 13347 | 142 ms | 27 (A) / 27 (B) | 13271 / 7784 |
| P2 adaptive by connected WebRTC peers: numwant 3 while < 2, else 0 | 27 | 19254 | 150 ms | 36 (A) / 36 (B) | 19218 / 7762 |
| P3 = P2 + keepalive announce every INTERVAL/2 (per-instance setInterval cap) | 54 | 38564 | 159 ms | 72 (A) / 72 (B) | 38535 / 15591 |
| P4 count-aware probe-then-offer: numwant 0 until a tracker reports other peers (complete+incomplete-1), then 3 until min(2, others) WebRTC peers are connected | 0 | 1503 | 161 ms | 0 (A) / 0 (B) | 1503 / 972 |

Median size of one offer inside an announce frame (JSON, real Chromium 156 SDP with ICE complete, loopback host candidates): 691-742 bytes.

Reading: P1 (fixed `numwant` 2) removes 60% of the connections for free. P2 (stop offering once two WebRTC peers are connected) is weak in a two-peer swarm because each page has only one peer. **P3 is a counter-example: shortening the cadence without zeroing `numwant` doubles the cost.** **P4 (ask the tracker first, offer only if it reports others, stop at `min(2, others)` connected peers) creates no `RTCPeerConnection` at all while alone or while connected, sends 1.5 KB per 30 s to the trackers instead of 31 KB (-95%), and costs the joiner 16 ms (one extra tracker round trip) to its first wire.** With the stock parameters a swarm of >= 6 peers costs the tracker about 6 KB of egress per client per 2-minute cycle (5 offers x 0.69 KB + answers); P4 cuts that to a few hundred bytes. Edge cases to keep: a lone visitor must still *answer* (it does; answers are not governed by `numwant`); ghost peers in a public tracker's count make P4 degrade to the stock behaviour (bounded); ICE failures are retried on the next cycle because `connected < min(2, others)` still holds.

**Recommended client policy (R5) in pseudo-code** (`src/loader/torrent.js`, after `client.add`):
```js
const st = { others: null, lastKick: 0 }
const webrtc = () => torrent.wires.filter(w => w.type === 'webrtc').length
client.tracker.getAnnounceOpts = () => ({ numwant:
  st.others === null ? 0                                   // first announce: probe only
  : webrtc() >= Math.min(2, st.others) ? 0 : 3 })          // else at most 3 offers per tracker
torrent.discovery.tracker.on('update', r => {              // complete + incomplete includes ourselves
  st.others = (r.complete || 0) + (r.incomplete || 0) - 1
  if (st.others > 0 && webrtc() < Math.min(2, st.others) && Date.now() - st.lastKick > 3000) { st.lastKick = Date.now(); torrent.discovery.tracker.update() }
})
for (const t of torrent.discovery.tracker._trackers) {     // internal API, pinned and e2e-tested
  const base = t.setInterval.bind(t); t.setInterval = ms => base(Math.min(ms ?? 30000, 55000)); t.setInterval(55000)  // keepalive cadence, offers stay at numwant 0
  t._startReconnectTimer = function () { this.reconnecting = true; clearTimeout(this.reconnectTimer)
    this.reconnectTimer = setTimeout(() => { this.retries++; this._openSocket() }, 500 + Math.random() * Math.min(60000, 1000 * 2 ** this.retries)) }
}
const kick = () => { for (const t of torrent.discovery.tracker._trackers) if (t.reconnecting) { clearTimeout(t.reconnectTimer); t.retries = 0; t._openSocket() }; torrent.discovery.tracker.update() }
addEventListener('online', kick); document.addEventListener('visibilitychange', () => document.visibilityState === 'visible' && kick())
```
**How many trackers.** Four to five entries: own neutral-host tracker(s) first, then `tracker.webtorrent.dev`, `tracker.openwebtorrent.com`, `tracker.novage.com.ua` (+ `open.ftorrent.com`). Under P4 each extra entry costs one ~250-byte keepalive frame per 55 s and, only while discovering, up to 3 offers; under the stock policy each extra entry costs 5 connections per cycle, which is why the stock client should never be shipped with 5 trackers on phones. Hard rules from the first wave still apply: identical list for every peer (a peer that shares no tracker with another never meets it), canonical spelling (trailing slashes are stripped and de-duplicated by the client), no `ws://`, no non-443 ports, no `files.fm`, no PeerTube trackers.

**Precedent.** Trystero 0.25.4 (2026-08-30): "Rooms retain their fast startup announcement burst, then switch to a slower steady cadence"; torrent strategy `offersPerAnnounce = 1`, `requestedPeers = 3`, `defaultAnnounceMs = 10 s`, `dormantAnnounceMs = 120 s`, honours tracker intervals (so P4's idea of fewer offers is mainstream); 0.26.0: "creating connection offers on demand instead of keeping prewarmed peer connections". P2P Media Loader 4.0.0 defaults `webRtcOffersCount = 5` (the same cost as stock WebTorrent). PeerTube's tracker limits announces to 15 per IP per infohash per 5 minutes when `reject_too_many_announces` is on. ftorrent's operators report that rejecting traffic made load worse because clients retried at once: every retry in our shim is jittered and capped.

**Consequences for the loader.**
1. `publish.mjs`/`magnetFromRecord` must always emit `ws=` and `xs=` for production sites, and `docs/DEPLOY.md` must state that the web-seed host needs CORS for **both** the site files and `<ws base>/<name>.torrent` (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin`, `Access-Control-Allow-Headers: range, cache-control`, `Access-Control-Expose-Headers: Content-Range, Content-Length, Accept-Ranges`; M4 of the mobile review).
2. A loader state "all trackers unreachable" must not be an error while a web seed works: show "Peers unavailable - loading from the web seed" (the `E_NO_PEERS` timeout of 60 s must only apply when there is neither `xs` nor `ws`). Tracker failures surface only as `warning` events (and `trackerAnnounce`/`noPeers` when they work), never as a rejection.
3. Do not wait for trackers before starting the HTTP path (it already does not).
4. Production precedent: PeerTube keeps playing over HTTP when its tracker/P2P is off ("If you disable the tracker, you disable the P2P on your PeerTube instance").
5. Test it: add the C/D/E scenarios to the end-to-end suite (`e2e/degrade.mjs` is a ready starting point).
**Confidence:** high (reproducible, all combinations run, three failure modes distinct). Not tested: slow (not dead) web seed, Range-less hosts (the review's B11), Safari's `AbortSignal.timeout` limits on old iOS.

## Gotchas (new in this wave; first-wave gotchas G1-G24 in `02-wss-trackers.md` still apply)

1. **One message kills a stock `bittorrent-tracker` 11.2.3 server** (`offers:[null]` with another peer in the swarm; uncaught `TypeError` in a `nextTick` callback, exit code 1). PeerTube's server-only fork is byte-identical.
2. **The first-wave snippet was wrong:** `import { Server } from 'bittorrent-tracker/server'` fails; the module has a **default** export (`import Server from 'bittorrent-tracker/server'`).
3. **The library removes only the first `peer_id` of a socket when it closes**; a client that varies `peer_id` leaves ghost peers for 20 minutes (and their swarms are never deleted). The wrapper pins one `peer_id` per connection.
4. **`/stats` and `/stats.json` are synchronous and O(peers)** and are on by default in the CLI: measured 76 ms for the first request and 30-32 ms afterwards with 10,000 peers in 1,000 swarms, i.e. the event loop is blocked that long on every request (linear extrapolation: ≈ 0.3 s at 100,000 peers, unverified). Do not expose them; export counters instead.
5. **Per-socket output backlog x connection cap is the real memory ceiling.** Under a slow-reader load the stock server reached 665-955 MiB with 4,000 sockets; the first wrapper default (1 MiB) reached 0.7-1.7 GB. Default is now 256 KiB; keep `MemoryMax` in the unit as backstop.
6. **Caddy:** `servers { metrics }` is deprecated in 2.11.x (use global `metrics`); `caddy reload` closes every WebSocket after 71 ms (code 1001), `stream_close_delay` only postpones it (8,049 ms measured for `8s`); `servers.timeouts.idle` does not affect upgraded sockets (survived 40 s with `idle 10s`); `respond "text\n"` prints a literal `\n`.
7. **Block lists:** HaGeZi Light/Multi/Pro/Pro++/Ultimate block `broker.emqx.io` and `broker.hivemq.com`; HaGeZi Anti-Piracy blocks all four public trackers and BlockListProject "torrent" two of them (openwebtorrent, novage); BlockListProject "tracking" blocks `stun.l.google.com`; EasyPrivacy blocks third-party WebSockets to `duckdns.org`; uBO's Unbreak list carries a legacy exception for `tracker.btorrent.xyz`. A local sinkhole (`broker.hivemq.com` -> `0.0.0.0` on the development Mac) produces false "down" results: resolve through DoH when probing.
8. **Probe hygiene:** a local load test (8-12k loopback connections plus a TIME_WAIT backlog on macOS's 16,384-port ephemeral range) made a soak sample fail for three operators at once and even made the fetch tool fail with `EADDRNOTAVAIL`. Excluded samples are listed with the reason; the raw JSONL is untouched.
9. **WebTorrent store:** the default browser chunk store (OPFS) failed with `UnknownError` on WebKit 27.2 (Playwright) during the first web-seed piece; a custom in-memory store passes. Not a tracker problem, but it looks like one in `E_NO_PEERS` handling.
10. **Cloudflare Durable Objects:** one socket terminates in exactly one object (swarm in the URL); deploys disconnect all sockets; in-memory state is lost on hibernation (attachments only, 16,384 bytes); incoming messages billed 20:1; hibernation eligibility is lost with `setTimeout`/`setInterval`/alarms/outgoing sockets; Free plan = SQLite-backed objects only; enforcement of the 20:1 rule on Free is disputed (user report, unverified).
11. **Fly.io:** no free tier for new orgs; default concurrency `type = connections` with `soft_limit` 20 if unset; `auto_stop_machines` must stay off; TLS ends at the Fly proxy so the client IP is in `Fly-Client-IP` (the wrapper needs `TRUST_ANY_PROXY=1` + `CLIENT_IP_HEADER`).
12. **Oracle Always Free:** 2 OCPU / 12 GB (A1), 10 TB/month egress, home region only, "out of host capacity" errors, idle reclamation after 7 days of CPU p95 < 20% and network < 20% and memory < 20% (A1), stateless security-list rules above ~40k peers (first wave).
13. **systemd:** `MemoryDenyWriteExecute=yes` breaks V8; the reference unit (seccomp `@system-service` minus `@privileged @resources`, empty capability set, `ProtectSystem=strict`) runs Node 24.22.0 fine and scores **3.3 "OK"** in `systemd-analyze security` on Ubuntu 24.04.5.
14. **Monitoring SaaS:** no WebSocket monitor type on the UptimeRobot or Better Stack pricing pages; UptimeRobot's free plan is described as "good for hobby and non-profit projects" (check the terms before relying on it commercially); GitHub `schedule` may be delayed or dropped and auto-disables after 60 days of inactivity.
15. **Vendor pages:** Hetzner's plan prices are rendered client-side (not retrievable here; the plan pages also showed every EU shared plan as "currently unavailable" at fetch time, possibly a rendering artefact); Vultr's pricing page returns 403 to non-browser clients.
16. **Tooling limits met during this research:** the shared WebSearch budget (200 per turn) ran out mid-run; GitHub's unauthenticated REST API (60 requests/hour per IP) was exhausted (by this job and possibly others on the same address); Playwright's Firefox build 1555 would not start on this machine; macOS TCC blocks OrbStack VMs from reading `~/Documents` (a stuck prompt shows as a hang).
17. **Trystero's torrent strategy** sends 1 offer and requests 3 peers per announce while WebTorrent sends up to 5: peers of different libraries on the same tracker swarm behave differently.
18. **Server-side counts include the caller:** `complete + incomplete` in an announce response counts the announcing peer; "others" = that minus 1.
